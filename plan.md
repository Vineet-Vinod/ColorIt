# Finetuning Plan

The current baseline pipeline built around DeOldify is performant and very good at generating a baseline coloring of B/W movies. The main problem left to solve is the costume colors. The model loves using faded and dark colors for costumes with no creativity, which diminishes the quality and viewing experience. Several experiments were run, including finetuning the base DeOldify model (decoder only, encoder + decoder), adding a color correction step and training a model for that and attempting to incorporate other papers. However, all of them had subpar results. This is a collection of ideas that were tried and failed, and promising directions for future work. 

1. Warm / Aggressive Postprocess Variants: Several config-only passes pushed warmth and blue reduction harder in an attempt to fix costumes without retraining. They changed the tone of the whole frame, but they did not reliably fix the actual clothing failures and often traded one bias for another.
2. DeOldify Finetune Runs: Multiple DeOldify finetunes were trained to reduce blue bias and learn richer period clothing colors from color reference films. None of them produced a trustworthy improvement on the real failure case, so they added training and checkpoint complexity without earning a place in the pipeline.
3. Actor Mask Backend Experiments: Different actor masking backends were compared to localize edits to people and reduce background spill. The conservative Mask R-CNN style backend was the best of that group and improved localization, but masking quality alone did not fix the wrong costume palette.
4. Masked Refiner Experiments: The masked refiner path did achieve much better localization than the unmasked versions and reduced spill substantially. The problem was that it still learned the wrong color family, so the model stayed green/cyan-biased even when it was editing the right region.
5. 5-Channel Refiner Variants: The person-mask and actor-aligned costume-mask refiners were attempts to give the model stronger structural guidance. They became more localized than older versions, but both still failed the core visual test: costumes remained dull or shifted into the wrong green/cyan range instead of becoming vivid and period-plausible.
6. Warm-Red Focused Refiner: One training run explicitly pushed the refiner toward warm reds and maroons to match the sari failure case. The metrics moved in the intended direction, but the rendered frames did not, which was a strong sign that the supervision and representation were still wrong for the job.
7. Exemplar / Reference Color Transfer: [Deep Exemplar-style](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Zhang_Deep_Exemplar-Based_Video_Colorization_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf) reference transfer was tested because it looked like the best paper match for same-era color references. In practice it was too weak and too global: it dulled the frame or transferred palette tendencies without giving controlled, vivid garment recoloring.
8. Costume Palette Guidance: The mask-only costume palette stage was the first experiment that clearly moved some costumes in the right direction without wrecking the whole frame. It proved that localized costume correction on top of DeOldify is the right shape of solution, but it was far too slow and still depended on weak garment masks.
9. Costume Hint Recolor: The Levin-style hint propagation pass improved over the simple palette wash by preserving more cloth shading and reducing some spill. It still failed on the two real blockers, though: incomplete costume coverage and bad garment segmentation, which caused partial recolors, repeated colors across characters, and bleed into adjacent regions.

What Actually Looked Promising: Conservative mask R-CNN was good at identifying actors and their costumes. Only coloring in the masks moved in a positive direction, but it wasn't sufficient (costumes looked faded in the target color) and the color bled into the actors.

The goal is to use these learnings, and come up with more experiments and ideas to recover vivid costume colors without affecting the rest of the scene (actors and backgrounds since DeOldify does an amazing job at coloring them).
